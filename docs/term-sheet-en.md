# Term sheet: baseload PPA, 20 MW solar, Puglia (offer B)

Non-binding illustration of the recommended offer from the app's Puglia example (zone SUD), on real ENTSO-E prices. **All price and volume figures are indicative and depend on stated assumptions** (trailing 12-month baseload reference of 148.1 EUR/MWh, a +-30% price-level band, imbalance cost of 6.35 EUR/MWh). Indicative, educational, built on public data. Not trading advice and not any company's pricing.

| Term | Content | Why it matters to the customer |
|---|---|---|
| Parties | Seller: the owner of the Puglia solar park (fictional). Buyer: an A-rated counterparty (fictional). | The buyer's rating drives credit risk: A means a lower default probability than the BBB and BB buyers in offers A and C. The modelled expected credit loss is small (about 1,400 EUR in present value). |
| Asset | 20 MWp ground-mounted solar park, Tavoliere near Foggia, SUD zone; P50 about 29,335 MWh/yr (PVGIS, indicative). | The contract must be tied to a defined, metered asset so that availability and curtailment are allocated clearly. |
| Structure | Baseload block: the buyer pays the fixed price on a constant hourly volume every hour of the year; the plant's output is sold at the day-ahead zonal price and the difference between the block and the output settles at that price. | The seller gets a fixed revenue on the block but keeps the shape risk: in sunny hours output exceeds the block and sells at low prices, in dark hours the seller must buy the shortfall at the zonal price. |
| Volume | Block of 1.67 MW (14,670 MWh/yr), about 50% of the plant's average output of 3.35 MW. The model sizes the hedge at 100% of production under this offer, that is the full block as offered; output above the block stays at spot. | The block is the hedge. A block that is too large leaves the seller short in low-sun hours at high prices. |
| Price | 140.7 EUR/MWh, 95% of the 148.1 EUR/MWh trailing 12-month baseload level of the zone (indicative). The merchant break-even price is 142.1 EUR/MWh: at the offer price the contract gives up about 0.4% of expected value (NPV 28.96 vs 29.08 M EUR) and lifts the average of the worst 10% of scenarios from 18.7 to 22.1 M EUR. | The price is the cost of the downside protection. The customer should judge whether 3.4 M EUR more in the bad tail is worth a small loss in the average. |
| Indexation | 2% per year on the price. | Indexation keeps the real value of the price over seven years. Whether the buyer accepts it affects the other terms. |
| Tenor | 7 years. | Shorter than offers A (10 years) and C (12 years): less lock-in and a shorter exposure to one buyer, but visibility for fewer years. |
| Delivery point | Day-ahead price of the SUD bidding zone, settled financially on the metered production and the block. | The zonal price, not the national price, reflects the local solar effect (cannibalisation) that the asset actually earns. |
| Imbalance | Stays with the seller. | Forecast errors cost the seller about 6.35 EUR/MWh (10% forecast miss at the single imbalance price, last 24 months). Offers that move imbalance to the buyer carry a balancing fee in the model (an example assumption of 110% of that cost). |
| GoO | Included: the seller transfers the guarantees of origin to the buyer. | The seller gives up this revenue stream (placeholder value 1 EUR/MWh) and should check that the price compensates it. |
| Negative prices | No special clause: the block is a fixed volume. | The seller is exposed to hours with a day-ahead price at or below zero on output above the block; the customer should check how many such hours the zone has. |
| Credit support | To be agreed: for an A-rated buyer, a rating-trigger clause that requires a guarantee if the rating falls. | Protects the seller in the unlikely case of a downgrade, without the cost of support from day one. |
| Termination | Termination payment on early end (mark-to-market, both ways), cure periods, and events of default including buyer insolvency and rating downgrade. | Defines what the seller receives if the buyer defaults or exits, which is the main credit risk of the contract. |
| Change in law | Renegotiation or adjustment if a change in law or regulation materially changes the economics, including the phase-out of the national single price (PUN) and changes to market rules; status to be verified on GME / ARERA before signing. | Italian market rules are changing. The customer needs a clear rule on who bears the change, rather than a dispute later. |
| Governing law | Italian law, courts or arbitration to be agreed. | Predictable enforcement for an Italian asset and counterparty. |
