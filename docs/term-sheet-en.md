# Term sheet: floor PPA, 20 MW solar, Puglia (offer C)

Non-binding illustration of the recommended offer from the app's Puglia example. **All price and volume figures are indicative, to be updated with the real-data run** (the current run uses a synthetic placeholder price series). Indicative, educational, built on public data. Not trading advice and not any company's pricing.

| Term | Content | Why it matters to the customer |
|---|---|---|
| Parties | Seller: the owner of the Puglia solar park (fictional). Buyer: a BB-rated counterparty (fictional). | The buyer's rating drives credit risk: BB means a higher default probability than the A or BBB buyers in offers A and B. |
| Asset | 20 MWp ground-mounted solar park, Tavoliere near Foggia, SUD zone; P50 about 29,335 MWh/yr (PVGIS, indicative). | The contract volume must be tied to a defined, metered asset so that availability and curtailment are allocated clearly. |
| Structure | Floor: the seller receives the higher of the day-ahead zonal price and the floor price, and pays a fixed premium for the floor. | The seller keeps the upside of high prices and is protected below the floor. The cost is the premium. |
| Volume | About 44% of metered production (about 12,800 MWh/yr at P50; indicative, to be updated). The rest stays merchant. | Selling only part of the output limits credit exposure to this buyer and keeps price upside on the remainder. |
| Price | Floor at 70% of the P50 capture price (about 64 EUR/MWh; indicative, to be updated). Premium 4 EUR/MWh on contracted volume (indicative). | The floor fixes the worst-case price. The premium is a certain cost, so the customer should compare it with the downside the floor removes. |
| Indexation | None (flat floor and premium for the whole tenor). | A flat floor loses real value over 12 years if inflation is positive. The customer should know this and may ask for indexation. |
| Tenor | 12 years. | A long tenor gives revenue visibility but locks in the terms. It also lengthens exposure to a BB buyer. |
| Delivery point | Day-ahead price of the SUD bidding zone, settled financially on metered production. | The zonal price, not the national price, reflects the local solar effect (cannibalisation) that the asset actually earns. |
| Imbalance | Buyer takes imbalance responsibility. | Forecast errors cost the seller nothing. The model assumes about 3 EUR/MWh of imbalance cost that the customer avoids (placeholder assumption). |
| GoO | Not included. The seller keeps the guarantees of origin and sells them separately (placeholder value 1 EUR/MWh). | The seller keeps this revenue stream and can sell it to the best bidder. |
| Negative prices | Zero volume in hours with a day-ahead price at or below zero. | The seller is not paid for output in hours when the market pays to take power. The customer should check how many such hours the zone has. |
| Credit support | To be agreed: for a BB buyer, a parent guarantee or bank guarantee sized to the exposure, with step-up if the rating falls. | Without support, a buyer default leaves the seller on the merchant market at the worst moment. |
| Termination | Termination payment on early end (mark-to-market, both ways), cure periods, and events of default including buyer insolvency and rating downgrade. | Defines what the seller receives if the buyer defaults or exits, which is the main credit risk of the contract. |
| Change in law | Renegotiation or adjustment if a change in law or regulation materially changes the economics, including the phase-out of the national single price (PUN) and changes to market rules; status to be verified on GME / ARERA before signing. | Italian market rules are changing. The customer needs a clear rule on who bears the change, rather than a dispute later. |
| Governing law | Italian law, courts or arbitration to be agreed. | Predictable enforcement for an Italian asset and counterparty. |
